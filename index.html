The MG53 Family of Companies

One protein.
Four companies, one repair.

Every cell depends on its membrane, and every membrane eventually fails. Dr. Jianjie Ma discovered MG53, the body's own repair protein, and helped found TRIM-edicine in 2008 to develop it. His laboratory at the University of Virginia later advanced that science further, and three more companies grew out of that work, each carrying it forward in its own way.

The Common Thread

It begins at the membrane.

Every cell is held by a membrane, a boundary just a few molecules thick that decides what stays, what enters, and whether the cell survives. When that boundary tears, or when the immune system meant to guard it turns destructive instead, disease follows. The four companies in this family all trace back to the same finding: the body already has a way to answer this. The work is in delivering it.

HTIC

A humanized antibody, downstream of the damage.

hCitH3-mAb binds and neutralizes citrullinated Histone H3, interrupting the NET-driven feedback loop that turns the immune system's first responders into the source of organ failure in sepsis and ARDS. FDA IND cleared in 2025.

MG Therapeutics

The repair protein itself, given directly.

MG53 is a naturally occurring protein that rushes to a membrane injury and seals it. MG Therapeutics develops MG53-based therapeutics across muscle, heart, lung, and kidney, wherever membrane injury and chronic inflammation take hold.

EV53

The same protein, carried by the body's own vehicles.

EV53 engineers extracellular vesicles, membrane-bound carriers the body already produces, to deliver MG53 where it's needed. The carrier and the cure share one nature: a membrane that protects, and a protein that mends.

TRIM-edicine

The founding platform, since 2008.

TRIM-edicine was built around Dr. Ma's original discovery of MG53 and now develops it alongside two related proteins, MG29 and ATAP, spanning muscle aging, tissue repair, and oncology.
